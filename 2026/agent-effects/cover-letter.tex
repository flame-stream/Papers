\noindent Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript, ``When Tool Calls Succeed but Workflows Fail:
Anomalies at the Agent--Tool Boundary,'' for consideration as a regular
paper in \emph{Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems}.

LLM-based agents increasingly act through tools supplied by independent
parties. Individual tool calls can succeed while their combined effects
leave the world in a state the workflow never intended: an action happens
twice, an aborted branch leaves residue, or an outside party reacts to an
effect that is later withdrawn. Retries, speculative execution, and
concurrency among agents all lead to such outcomes.

The paper gives an effect-history model that separates what happened in
the world from what the agent observed, and a catalog of eight anomalies
grouped by outcome uncertainty, workflow structure, and interaction with
other agents or outside observers. For each anomaly we identify the
capability the tool boundary must provide to exclude it, and we describe
four limits beyond which no agent runtime can provide a guarantee
without additional boundary support, such as authoritative tool
participation, shared mediation, or visibility control. A census of 98,291 tools from the Model Context
Protocol registry shows that the standard annotation vocabulary expresses none of
these capabilities in full.

We believe the paper fits the journal's interest in the specification and
verification of agent systems. It treats a tool as an independently operated
service whose call interface need not define a shared protocol for resolving
outcomes, and relates our
capabilities to work on fault-tolerant information protocols and
commitments in the multiagent-systems literature. The required
information sheet is attached.

Data, code, and methodology are available at\\
\url{https://github.com/flame-stream/mcp-annotation-census}\\
and a preprint at\\
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15397}.

An earlier, shorter version has been accepted at ``Who Verifies the
Agents?'', a non-archival workshop at NeurIPS 2026 (December 2026) that does not publish
proceedings. This manuscript revises it based on the workshop reviews.

Apart from the preprint noted above, the manuscript has not been
published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors have read and approved this submission and
declare no competing interests.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
