\noindent Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript, ``When Tool Calls Succeed but Workflows Fail:
Anomalies at the Agent--Tool Boundary,'' for consideration in
\emph{Computer Science and Information Systems}.

Information systems increasingly delegate work to AI agents that act
through services supplied by independent parties: they send messages,
place orders, and charge cards. Individual service calls can succeed while
their combined effects leave the system in a state the workflow never
intended: an action happens twice, an aborted branch leaves residue, or an
outside party reacts to an effect that is later withdrawn. Retries,
speculative execution, and concurrent executions all lead to such
outcomes.

The paper gives an effect-history model that separates what happened in
the world from what the agent observed, and a catalog of eight anomalies
grouped by outcome uncertainty, workflow structure, and interaction with
other executions or outside observers. For each anomaly we identify the
capability the tool boundary must provide to exclude it, and we describe
four limits beyond which no agent runtime can provide a guarantee
without additional boundary support, such as authoritative tool
participation, shared mediation, or visibility control. A census of
98,291 tools from the Model Context Protocol registry shows that the
standard annotation vocabulary expresses none of these capabilities in
full.

We believe the paper fits the journal's scope in information systems and
software engineering. It combines a theoretical analysis, building on
transaction models such as sagas and multilevel transactions, with an
empirical study of a widely used service interface.

Data, code, and methodology are available at\\
\url{https://github.com/flame-stream/mcp-annotation-census}\\
and a preprint at\\
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15397}.

An earlier version has been accepted at ``Who Verifies the
Agents?'', a non-archival workshop at NeurIPS 2026 (December 2026) that
does not publish proceedings. This manuscript revises it based on the
workshop reviews.

Apart from the preprint noted above, the manuscript has not been
published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors have
read and approved this submission. We are not aware of any conflicts of
interest with the editors or potential reviewers.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
