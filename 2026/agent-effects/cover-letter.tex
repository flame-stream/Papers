\noindent Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, ``When Tool Calls Succeed but Workflows Fail:
Anomalies at the Agent--Tool Boundary,'' to the \emph{Journal of Intelligent
Information Systems}.

The paper studies a problem in agent workflows: individual tool calls can
succeed, while their combined effects leave an incorrect result. Retries,
partial failures, speculative execution, and concurrent actions can all lead
to this. Transaction processing offers ways to handle such problems, but
these depend on properties that independently supplied tools often do not
expose.

We present an effect-history model and a catalog of eight anomalies. We
identify the tool capabilities needed to prevent them and discuss four
limits on what a runtime can guarantee without support from the tools. We
also examine the standard MCP annotations across 98,291 tools. These
annotations provide some useful hints, but do not fully express the
capabilities needed for these guarantees.

We think JIIS is a good fit because the paper connects
transaction-processing concepts with the reliability of AI agents. Its main
contribution is an analysis of the contracts needed between agents and
tools, supported by measurements of an existing interface.

The data, code, and methodology are available at:\\
\url{https://github.com/flame-stream/mcp-annotation-census}

A preprint is available at:\\
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15397}

An earlier version was accepted at ``Who Verifies the Agents?'', a
non-archival workshop at NeurIPS 2026 that does not publish proceedings.
This manuscript revises it based on the workshop reviews.

The manuscript has not been published in a journal and is not under
consideration by another journal. All authors have read and approved this
submission and declare no competing interests.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
